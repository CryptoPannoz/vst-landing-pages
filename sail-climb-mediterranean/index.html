The problem

Most climbing trips waste your week on logistics.

Three weeks of planning. A rental car that costs more than the flight. Spots you can't reach without a boat. Half the days gone before you've touched rock.

01

Planning is a second job

Researching crags, securing huts, juggling guides — your free week disappears into spreadsheets before you tie your shoes.

02

The best crags are unreachable

The most spectacular walls in the Mediterranean don't have a trail. No parking lot. No access by car. Only by boat. And you don't have one.

03

Your week gets over-ridden

Transfers. Hotel check-ins. Rental car returns. Restaurant searches. Drive times. By the end of seven days you've climbed maybe three.

Our answer

A floating base camp. You show up. We do the rest.

The sailboat isn't a gimmick. It's the access tool. Every morning, it's already anchored under the wall you're going to climb. You don't drive. You don't transfer. You wake up on the rock.

Before you ask

The four questions we hear every week.

What does the price include? +

Accommodation on the sailing boat, a professional Skipper and a certified Mountain Guide, pantry-ready service, three nights in the harbor, boat fuel — and a Vertical Sailing Tour t-shirt.

Not included: travel to and from the harbor, meals on land, and the €300 onboard payment, collected in cash at boarding to cover shared pantry and boat expenses with full transparency.

Can I join without climbing experience? +

Yes. A Mountain Guide is on board to teach climbing techniques, rope maneuvers and safety procedures.

Crags are selected with a wide range of grades, so everyone finds routes that match their level — beginners climb with full guidance, experienced climbers push their grade. Deep water soloing is best suited for climbers comfortable on 6b or higher.

What gear should I bring? +

Climbing shoes, harness, helmet and chalk bag. One 70m single rope and a set of 12 quickdraws are enough for a climbing team — and both can be rented for the week for €80.

Pack everything in a soft bag (no rigid suitcases on board), with hiking shoes, a swimsuit, a warm layer and sunscreen. Sheets, blankets and towels are provided.

How does a private tour work? +

You customize everything: dates, destination, boat, onboard professionals and itinerary. A private tour typically becomes cost-effective from 6 guests, but smaller groups and couples are possible too.

You can add a hostess/cook for meals and cleaning, and highly experienced groups can opt to sail without a guide to lower costs. Non-climbers are welcome: snorkeling, trekking, beach life and chilling on board. See the private tour page →
